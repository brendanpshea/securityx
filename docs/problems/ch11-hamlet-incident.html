Chapter 11

Hamlet Investigates the Insider That Dwelled (Diagnostic)

Four months into Hamlet's rebuilt detection program at Denmark Cyber Defense, the executive dashboard tells a flattering story: average MTTD is down 38%, the auto-acknowledged-alert rate is under 12%, and the detection-as-code repo has 41 contributors. Two distinct alerts have already led to confirmed contained incidents.

Then, on a Tuesday morning, Horatio Communications — a customer — calls. They have evidence that a year's worth of their proprietary research has been sold to a competitor by a former Denmark Cyber Defense employee who left amicably six weeks ago.

Walking the telemetry backward, Hamlet finds that the signals were all there. The UEBA had flagged the employee's peer-relative behavior twice. A Sigma rule had fired weeks earlier on a related indicator. An ISAC bulletin two weeks before the exfil began had named the exact staging-domain pattern the employee eventually used. Every one of these had been triaged — and dismissed — because nothing had connected them.

Detection retrospective — Insider exfil case INC-2026-117.   Author: Hamlet, Detection Engineering Lead. Status: draft for SOC leadership and Horatio Communications counsel.

What we missed, and when

  • Day −393 (~13 months before exfil discovered). UEBA anomaly score on emp-073: download volume from research-share-prod exceeded the peer-group baseline by 4.2σ. Alert opened, severity Low. Closed within 9 minutes with note: 'finance access — normal for role.' The note was wrong; the employee was in research, not finance.
  • Day −51. Sigma rule T1041-staging-upload-anomaly fires twice on the same workstation. The rule's published FPR was 64% — analysts had learned to dismiss it without investigating. Auto-ack triggered after analyst marked it 'noise.'
  • Day −14. ISAC bulletin MS-ISAC-2026-04-118 arrives via TAXII. It describes — by sector — exactly this attack: staging domain pattern *-research-mirror[0-9]\.[a-z]+\.cc, file naming proj-<5-digit>.tar.gz. The IOCs sat in the raw feed. Nothing ever correlated them with the workstation events from Day −51.
  • Day 0 → Day +28. ~14 GB of research data exfiltrated, in 200 MB nightly bursts, to research-mirror02.synapse-int.cc. Telemetry captured every transfer.
  • Day +28. Employee resigns. Offboarding clean. No flags on the way out.
  • Day +70 (today). Horatio Communications calls.

What Hamlet now knows

The data was there. The detections existed. The hunt — the question that would have joined these three signals into one investigation — was never asked. The dashboard reads green because the program optimized for the things it had decided to measure.

For each missed signal in the retrospective, name the specific detection-engineering control or discipline that failed.

Two relevant alerts fired and were closed because no one had a guiding question that connected them. In threat-hunting terms, this is a failure of — the discipline of starting an investigation from a specific testable claim about adversary behavior rather than from whichever alert happens to be at the top of the queue.

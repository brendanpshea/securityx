Chapter 1

Snow White Builds an Awareness Program

Snow White has just been promoted to GRC Analyst at Seven Dwarfs Mining, a 400-person mining and refining company headquartered in the Black Forest. Last quarter, Grumpy clicked a link in an email claiming to be from the Queen's Royal Bank, exposing payroll data for the entire mining crew. The CEO, Doc, has asked Snow White to stand up a formal security awareness program before the next audit.

A junior analyst has handed Snow White a draft document mixing several governance tiers together in one file. Before Snow White can finalize the program, she has to split this draft into the correct document types — and pick the right metrics, framework, and ownership tool for the program itself.

The audit committee has signaled they care most about IT process maturity rather than ITSM service delivery.

Excerpt — Awareness Program Draft (mixed tiers)

  1. Seven Dwarfs Mining is committed to protecting employee and customer information from unauthorized disclosure or loss. Senior management endorses this commitment.
  2. All employees must complete security-awareness training within 30 days of hire and annually thereafter. All employee passwords must be a minimum of 14 characters and rotated every 90 days.
  3. Recommended: Use a passphrase of four or more random words rather than a complex random string — it is easier to recall and mathematically harder to crack.
  4. Help Desk — Resetting a locked account: (a) verify employee identity via callback to the number stored in HR; (b) in Active Directory, right-click the user → Reset Password; (c) force password change at next logon; (d) log the reset in the ITSM ticket.

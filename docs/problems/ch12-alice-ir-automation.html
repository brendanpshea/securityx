Alice Liddell Automates Incident Response at Wonderland Logistics

Alice Liddell, Incident Response Manager at Wonderland Logistics (a 6,000-employee regional freight company), is rebuilding the company's vulnerability management and incident response program after a credential-stuffing attack exposed 23 employee accounts. Her mandate covers three areas: closing the vulnerability pipeline gaps that let exposures accumulate undetected, deploying SOAR automation to compress response time, and establishing a forensic evidence process that can support disciplinary and legal action if a future incident involves an insider.

Alice also needs to present the program to the board using consistent, industry-standard terminology — CVSS scoring, NIST lifecycle framing, and measurable outcome metrics that replace the old vanity numbers.

T-101 is solved by prioritizing the 3,400 findings with the industry-standard , which produces a 0–10 numeric severity rating from metrics like attack vector, complexity, privileges required, and CIA impact. A network-exploitable, low-complexity flaw with no privileges required and full impact on confidentiality, integrity, and availability scores at or near the 10.0 maximum and jumps the queue regardless of the business unit's protest.

T-102 is solved by adopting the NIST suite of standards, specifically using documents to encode the CIS Linux Benchmark checklists that the scanner can evaluate automatically against all 1,200 hosts. The complementary language provides the system-state checks that determine whether each checklist item passes or fails on a given host.

To catch vulnerable third-party libraries before they ship to production, Alice mandates that every CI/CD pipeline produce a — a machine-readable inventory of every dependency — and that the pipeline fail the build when any entry in that inventory matches a critical CVE.

T-103 is solved by building a credential-stuffing response in the SOAR platform. When the SIEM fires the credential-stuffing alert pattern, the playbook automatically disables the flagged AD account, revokes all active cloud sessions, resets the password, and opens an incident ticket — compressing the 20-minute manual workflow to under 90 seconds. The complementary documents the manual steps an analyst follows for edge cases the automation cannot handle, such as when the flagged account belongs to an executive requiring HR sign-off before lockout.

For email-borne threats, Alice wires a phishing-detection playbook to the email gateway. Any suspicious message triggers the SOAR to extract headers and URLs, check them against the organization's , auto-quarantine the message fleet-wide, and push identified malicious domains and URLs to the — converting a detection into a containment action with no analyst involvement.

Alice frames the entire IR program around the classic four-phase . She opens every board presentation with two numbers: (how long an intrusion dwells before discovery) and (how long from discovery to containment) — replacing the previous vanity metric of total events ingested.

T-105 is exactly the failure mode the principle exists to prevent: before imaging the disk, the responder must first capture RAM contents, running process list, and open network connections — evidence that vanishes the moment the system is powered off. All captured evidence is hashed and documented in a form to maintain legal admissibility.

T-104 is exactly why Alice deploys an isolated detonation environment — a — so analysts can safely execute suspicious files and observe behavioral indicators (network connections, registry writes, process injection attempts) without risk to the production network. Indicators extracted from those detonation reports feed directly back into the SOAR's IOC-blocking pipeline.

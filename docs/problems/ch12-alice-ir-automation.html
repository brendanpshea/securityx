Alice Liddell Automates Incident Response at Wonderland Logistics

Alice Liddell, Incident Response Manager at Wonderland Logistics (a 6,000-employee regional freight company), is rebuilding the company's vulnerability management and incident response program after a credential-stuffing attack exposed 23 employee accounts. Her mandate covers three areas: closing the vulnerability pipeline gaps that let exposures accumulate undetected, deploying SOAR automation to compress response time, and establishing a forensic evidence process that can support disciplinary and legal action if a future incident involves an insider.

Alice also needs to present the program to the board using consistent, industry-standard terminology — CVSS scoring, NIST lifecycle framing, and measurable outcome metrics that replace the old vanity numbers.

Alice starts with the vulnerability backlog. The security scanner produces thousands of findings per month, and the engineering team can only remediate a fixed number per sprint. To prioritize rationally, Alice uses the industry-standard scoring system, which produces a 0–10 numeric severity rating from metrics like attack vector, complexity, privileges required, and CIA impact. A network-exploitable, low-complexity flaw with no privileges required and full impact on confidentiality scores near the maximum and jumps the queue regardless of the business unit's protest.

For machine-readable compliance scanning, Alice adopts the NIST suite, specifically using documents to encode CIS Benchmark hardening checklists that the scanner can evaluate automatically against every enrolled Linux host. The complementary language provides the system-state checks that determine whether each checklist item passes or fails on a given host.

To catch vulnerable third-party libraries before they ship to production, Alice mandates that every CI/CD pipeline produce a — a machine-readable inventory of every dependency — and that the pipeline fail the build when any entry in that inventory matches a critical CVE.

On the automation side, Alice builds a credential-stuffing response in the SOAR platform. When the SIEM fires the credential-stuffing alert pattern, the playbook automatically disables the flagged AD account, revokes all active cloud sessions, resets the password, and opens an incident ticket — compressing a 20-minute manual workflow to under 90 seconds. The complementary documents the manual steps an analyst follows for edge cases the automation cannot handle, such as when the flagged account belongs to an executive requiring HR sign-off before lockout.

For email-borne threats, Alice wires a phishing-detection playbook to the email gateway. Any suspicious message triggers the SOAR to extract headers and URLs, check them against the , auto-quarantine the message fleet-wide, and push identified malicious URLs to the DNS — converting a detection into a containment action with no analyst involvement.

Chapter 12

Alice Reviews the Hare Incident (Diagnostic)

Six months into Alice's IR-automation program at Wonderland Logistics, the metrics look strong. The credential-stuffing playbook has fired 87 times with no false positives — disabling accounts, revoking sessions, resetting passwords, opening tickets, all in under 90 seconds. MTTD on routine alerts is down 41%. Board approval, well earned.

Then a single case unravels everything.

A freight dispatcher — internal name march.hare@wonderland-logistics.com — was the subject of a customer ethics complaint. While HR was preparing an investigation, the SIEM fired a credential-stuffing alert on Hare's account; passwords from an unrelated 2024 LinkedIn dump had been replayed. The SOAR playbook executed normally: account disabled, all cloud sessions revoked, password reset, ticket opened. Routine. Ninety seconds. Green light on the dashboard.

The routine action destroyed the HR investigation. The session revocation purged Hare's cached browser tokens, his outbound email queue (with two attachments Legal had hoped to preserve), and his open file handles. Hare was alerted to the lockout by his phone two seconds later and immediately powered off his company laptop. By the time IR arrived to image the device, the RAM, running processes, open network connections, and unsaved chat client state were already gone.

A week later, an analyst opened the original suspicious .docx from the email-gateway phishing alert on her production workstation — 'just to see what was in it' — and triggered a beacon to an attacker-controlled C2. Two separate failures, one bad week.

Post-incident retrospective — case INC-2026-204 (Hare).   Author: A. Liddell, IR Manager. Status: draft for board audit committee and legal. Do not forward.

Sequence of events

  • 09:14:02 SIEM credential-stuffing alert on march.hare@wonderland-logistics.com. 23 login attempts from rotating IPs against the cloud-SSO endpoint.
  • 09:14:14 SOAR playbook creds-stuff-v3 fires. Account disabled, cloud sessions revoked, password reset, ticket opened. No human approval gate.
  • 09:14:16 Hare receives a Slack alert from the SSO portal: 'You have been signed out.' He powers off his laptop within 2 minutes — an action consistent with awareness of an ongoing investigation, but indistinguishable from a normal user reacting to suspicious activity.
  • 09:14–10:42 IR called in by HR. By the time the laptop is connected to the imaging station, RAM is unrecoverable. Two email attachments in the outbound queue have been purged by the session revocation step in the playbook. Slack DM history flagged for legal hold is intact; cached browser state used for two of Hare's research domains is not.
  • Day +6 Analyst opens the original suspicious .docx (from a separate case, INC-2026-198) on her production workstation. The file calls out to tea-party-c2.invalid and drops a payload. The host is quarantined within 14 minutes by EDR, but the analyst's email cache and saved credentials briefly exposed.

Contributing failures

  • The credential-stuffing playbook had no scoping condition for accounts flagged for active investigation, legal hold, or HR review. It fires the same on every alert.
  • Even where the playbook is the right response, there was no manual escalation path for high-sensitivity accounts — no procedure for don't auto-fire on these accounts; route to a human-led process instead.
  • IR was called twelve minutes after the laptop powered off. The capture order assumed the system was still on; nobody on shift had run this scenario in tabletop.
  • Captured evidence was hashed and logged, but the unbroken who-touched-what record between the playbook's actions and the IR team's image was never reconstructed for the legal hold.
  • The .docx from INC-2026-198 was opened on a production workstation. Wonderland Logistics has no isolated detonation environment; the analyst was 'trying to help.'
  • The dashboard's headline IR metrics — MTTD, MTTR — are real and improving. They do not show how often automation destroys the evidence it was meant to preserve.

The principle exists precisely because once the host is powered off, this evidence is unrecoverable; the IR team's capture order has to put it first, before disk imaging.

The credential-stuffing playbook fired identically on every alert regardless of account context. The fact that Hare was under HR investigation should have routed the response to a — the human-judgment counterpart to a playbook, where HR and legal sign-off precede any automated lockout that could destroy evidence.

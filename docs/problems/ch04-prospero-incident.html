Chapter 4

Prospero Reviews the Milan Island Breach (Diagnostic)

Six months after Prospero's Zero Trust redesign, the SASE rollout is the program's success story. 98% of applications sit behind ZTNA. The board dashboard is green. The auditors who had flagged the old castle-and-moat design signed off.

The 2% was a legacy ERP — Sycorax Financials — that could not speak SAML and could not be refactored before its vendor's end-of-support date. Rather than delay the whole migration, Prospero's team kept it reachable over a slimmed-down legacy VPN as a 'temporary exception, 90 days max.' That was eleven months ago.

A contractor laptop in São Paulo was compromised through a malicious browser extension. The attacker found the legacy VPN client still installed, connected, and reached Sycorax — and from Sycorax, the customer billing database. 2.1 million billing records left over four days. The ZTNA logs show nothing, because none of it went through ZTNA.

Post-incident report — Milan Island Telecom, Sycorax breach.   Author: Prospero, Network Architect. Status: draft for board and regulator notification. Do not forward.

How it happened

  • Day 0 Contractor laptop (São Paulo, unmanaged) compromised via a malicious browser extension. Endpoint posture: unknown — the device never enrolled because the contractor only ever needed the legacy VPN.
  • Day 0 Attacker finds the legacy VPN client still installed and configured. Authenticates with the contractor's cached VPN credentials. No posture check, no re-authentication, no device-trust evaluation.
  • Day 1 From the VPN's network position, attacker reaches sycorax-erp01. Sycorax sits on the original flat /16 — microsegmentation was applied to the new ZTNA-fronted workloads, never to the legacy segment.
  • Day 1–4 Lateral move from Sycorax to billing-db-prod. 2,143,910 records exfiltrated in nightly batches.
  • Day 6 Detected when a batch job's egress volume tripped a network bandwidth alert — not a security control.

Contributing failures

  • Sycorax was kept reachable over a legacy VPN as a 90-day exception. Eleven months later it was still there, still flat, still trusted-by-network-location.
  • The PDP was healthy and evaluating policy correctly — for every app that had a PEP in front of it. Sycorax had none; its requests never reached the PDP at all.
  • The legacy VPN authenticated the contractor once, at connect time, and never re-evaluated identity, device, or context for the rest of the multi-day session.
  • Microsegmentation was deployed for the new workloads. The legacy /16 — where Sycorax and the billing DB both lived — was never broken up. East-west movement between them was unrestricted.
  • The 2.1M-record export left without a DLP alert. Sycorax's exports carried no classification labels; the label-driven DLP rules had nothing to match on.
  • The board dashboard reported '98% of applications behind ZTNA.' The 2% was the entire breach path. Nobody was reporting on the assets the 2% could reach.

For each contributing failure in Prospero's report, name the Zero Trust principle or control that failed.

Keeping Sycorax reachable over a legacy VPN as an open-ended 'temporary exception' did not just defer work — it preserved exactly the thing Zero Trust exists to eliminate: an where being on the right network segment is treated as proof of trust.

The Policy Decision Point was healthy and evaluating policy correctly. It made no difference here because Sycorax had no in front of it — the component that intercepts a request and forces it through the PDP before the resource is reached. With no enforcement point, there was no decision to make.

The legacy VPN authenticated the contractor once at connect time and never again. This is the failure of — the Zero Trust principle that every request is re-evaluated against identity, device posture, and context, not granted for the life of a session.

The attacker moved freely from Sycorax to the billing database because the legacy /16 was never broken up. Microsegmentation was deployed for the new workloads only; this is an that left the highest-value data reachable from the one segment that had been left flat.

The 2.1-million-record export triggered no DLP alert because Sycorax's files carried no . Label-driven DLP can only match content that has been tagged; unlabeled legacy exports pass straight through.

Finally, the '98% behind ZTNA' headline is a vanity coverage number. The metric that would have surfaced this risk before it became a breach is — a measure weighted by which crown-jewel assets remain reachable outside the Zero Trust path, not by the percentage of apps that are inside it.

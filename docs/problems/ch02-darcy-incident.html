Chapter 2

Darcy Reviews the Pemberley Loss (Diagnostic)

Eight months after Darcy presented the Pemberley risk memo, the board approved the mitigation strategy on paper — segmenting Pemberley onto an isolated VLAN, deploying a GRC monitoring platform — and then deferred the funding to next fiscal year. Two priorities took precedence: a Salesforce migration and an acquisition Darcy's CEO was rumored to be courting.

In September, Pemberley Climate Services itself was compromised. A credential-stuffing attack against Pemberley's contractor VPN — which did not require MFA — gave the attacker a foothold. A few days later, an HVAC technician's laptop connected to the Derbyshire Holdings data-center LAN through the same cable Pemberley technicians had been using for five years. The attacker piggybacked, moved laterally across the flat /16 to PROD-FIN-01, and exfiltrated 180,432 cardholder records over six hours. No alert fired.

Darcy is now drafting the loss report for the board audit committee. The number on the bottom line is $4.8 million — 4.8 times the Single Loss Expectancy he modeled in Q1. His task is not to redo the risk calculation. It is to identify, for each contributing failure, which specific risk-management control failed — including the modeling itself.

Contributing failures

  • Risk register entry PCS-2025-04 was last reviewed 14 months before the incident. No interim re-assessment of Pemberley's posture; the deferred GRC platform never went in.
  • The board approved the mitigation strategy (segmentation + jump host) but deferred the funding to next fiscal. No documented acknowledgment that the residual exposure would be carried for another year. The mitigation existed only on paper.
  • Pemberley's poor patching record was already known from peer-industry reports published six months before the incident. Derbyshire never followed up; no contractual remediation was required.
  • The EF estimate (0.25) accounted for direct data-restoration cost only. Regulatory fines, customer churn, and the card-brand remediation program — all predictable line items for a payment-card breach — were not modeled.
  • 180,432 records left the network across six hours without triggering an alert. Egress telemetry was being collected; no pattern had ever been authored against it.
